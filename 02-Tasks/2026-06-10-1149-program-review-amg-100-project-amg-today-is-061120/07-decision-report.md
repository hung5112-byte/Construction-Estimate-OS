# Decision Report: AMG-100 Prototype Build Readiness & Key Risks

## Bottom Line (30-Second Summary)
We are 11 days away from starting our prototype build. We have only 3 days of spare time for the most critical part—reworking the antenna (part number ISS-014). If this slips, we miss our legal approval deadline.

We can keep the June 22 build date and the August 18 approval deadline by **committing $4,300 now** to strengthen our supply chain. All other costs will be set up as automatic triggers—spending that only happens if a specific problem actually occurs.

We are handling battery testing, engineering backup plans, and factory space using free actions and these conditional triggers, not by paying for insurance upfront. We explicitly reject two proposals to increase our test fleet size or speed up part qualification, as these add risk before we’ve proven the product is reliable. Every quality checkpoint is confirmed as non-negotiable. No test is skipped, no process is rushed, and the path to legal approval is fully protected.

## Recommendation
**Go, with adjustments**

## Detailed Analysis

### What Each Department Said

- **Engineering:** The prototype build is achievable but fragile. The rework parts for the antenna (ISS-014) are the single biggest schedule risk. The priority must be protecting the June 22 build slot.
- **New Product Introduction (NPI) & Program Management:** The antenna parts are due 11 days before the build, with a hard legal approval deadline on August 18 and zero spare time afterward. Several $0 actions, like assigning owners for returns and packaging, are already late.
- **Quality & Reliability:** Moving to the prototype build without confirming the battery fix and antenna compatibility carries unacceptable quality risk. Accelerated life testing (HALT) and formal document control are non-negotiable.
- **Manufacturing & Supplier Quality:** Two issues—aligning golden samples and fixing the outgoing quality sampling plan—are currently blocking progress. We can drive committed costs close to zero if free actions are closed this week. A small fix to a press-fit tool is the only factory cost that must be committed now.
- **Service Operations:** The antenna parts delay is the highest schedule risk. Getting the repair depot ready for this product is an unfunded, zero-cost task that must start immediately, or early customer returns in September will become dead units we cannot fix.

### The Core Debate: Pro vs. Con

The main debate was whether a trigger-based plan is enough for the ISS-014 antenna parts.

- **Pro Argument (Trigger-Based):** A fast global courier, activated only upon a confirmed delay, is the ideal math. It costs $0 now, with $800 expected only if fired, leaving our emergency fund untouched. A 24-hour courier is feasible, and our 3-day buffer is enough to absorb it.
- **Con Argument (Act Now):** Waiting for a delay confirmation before acting is a gamble, not a plan. A customs hold, a paperwork error on a battery component, or a simple missed connection can turn a "24-hour" courier into a three-day delay, burning our entire buffer. Having a courier already on-site is an insurance policy that buys real schedule margin against the program’s single biggest risk.
- **Resolution:** The Con argument is accepted. The $1,500 committed cost is a justified, direct solution to a schedule risk that could stop the entire program.

### Three Perspectives Considered

- **Growth Advocate:** Wanted to commit $24,000 to increase the test fleet and speed up component qualification to capture market share. **Rejected.** Scaling before proving reliability risks burning the trust of our Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)—the customer most likely to buy our product, who values reliability above all else.
- **Cautious Advocate:** Wanted $8,300 committed, including a $4,000 backup engineer standby fee and a larger pre-build. **Partially accepted.** The courier standby and overtime are kept; the backup engineer retainer is rejected in favor of a zero-cost documentation action.
- **Balanced (My View):** Integrates the best of all positions. Commits $4,300 to harden the critical supply chain and battery validation, uses free actions and triggers for all other risks, and rejects premature scaling.

## Must-Do Items Before Launch (Blockers)

- **ISS-014: Pre-stage a courier in Shenzhen.** The courier must be ready at the supplier's site by June 16, with the ability to ship dangerous goods.
- **Quality Gates:** Provide floor evidence that the measurement system is reliable and the outgoing sampling plan is corrected. Due June 16.
- **Repair Depot Owner:** Assign a single owner for the repair process. Due within 7 days.
- **Document Control:** Lock the prototype build procedure, antenna rework plan, and proof of quality under version control. Due June 16.

## Key Checkpoints (KPI Gates)

- **Week of June 16:** If rework parts are not received at our inspection center by June 19 noon, activate the fast courier trigger and pause all non-critical pre-build work to focus resources on getting the parts in.
- **Week of July 6:** A pre-assessment for a key compliance certification (PCI) occurs. Even one finding here is a major schedule risk.
- **Week of July 1:** The battery problem-solving process must be closed out. If not, we must conduct a deep-dive review of our trial spare strategy, as the rate of future field failures will remain a critical unknown.

## Decisions You Must Make

- **A:** Approve the $4,300 in committed spending (courier standby + battery overtime). **This is the recommendation.**
- **B:** Reject the courier standby and revert to a pure trigger-based approach ($0 committed, higher schedule risk).
- **C:** Adopt the Cautious plan ($8,300 committed, including the backup engineer standby fee).
- **D:** Adopt the Growth plan ($24,000 committed, including test fleet expansion).
